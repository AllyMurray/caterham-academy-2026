Nuts and bolts

Torque Checks

Work through these checks before the car goes on track. The list is grouped by area so it can be used around the car in a sensible workshop order. Check the support points before working underneath; Sigma tool references must match the fasteners on your car.

Front Suspension
  1. Upper wishbones: 47 Nm.Sigma reference: 17 mm socket and spanner. Sigma pp. 8–9.
  2. Lower wishbones: 81 Nm.Sigma reference: 19 mm socket and spanner. Sigma pp. 8–9.
  3. Dampers: 25 Nm.Sigma reference: 6 mm Allen key / hex bit. Sigma pp. 12–13.
  4. Top wishbone ball joints and locknuts: 54 Nm. Locknuts can only be hand-checked with a spanner.Partial Sigma reference: assembly lists 22 mm tools for ball-joint work; the locknut tool size is not separately identified. Sigma pp. 14–15.
  5. Brake calipers to hubs: 60 Nm.Tool size unconfirmed: the Sigma guide supplies the front brake/upright assembly preassembled and does not specify this fixing’s tool. Sigma pp. 14–15.
  6. Hubs to lower wishbones: 54 Nm.Partial Sigma reference: assembly lists ¾ inch and 22 mm tools, with different standard-track and wide-track fixings. Match the nut on your car. Sigma pp. 14–15.
  7. Wingstays to uprights: 81 Nm.Sigma reference: assembly lists 19 mm / ¾ inch tools for the ½ inch fixing. Check the fit on your nut. Sigma pp. 14–15.
  8. Wheel bearings: ensure wheels spin freely with no lateral play. Check the large centre nut with split pin.

Rear Suspension
  1. Radius arms to chassis: 47 Nm.Tools: 8 mm Allen key / hex bit and 17 mm socket. Radius-arm compatibility confirmed by the owner; a shortened 8 mm Allen key helps access. Sigma pp. 106–107.
  2. Radius arms to De Dion tube: 81 Nm.Tools: 19 mm socket and spanner. Radius-arm compatibility confirmed by the owner. Sigma pp. 106–107.
  3. De Dion tube aluminium endplate and bearing: 47 Nm.Sigma reference: 17 mm socket and spanners for the endplate / bearing-carrier fixings. Sigma pp. 114–115.
  4. Check welds on both De Dion tube and radius arms for cracking or fatigue.
  5. Dampers to De Dion tube: 81 Nm.Sigma reference: 19 mm socket for the lower damper mounting bolt. Sigma pp. 104–105.
  6. A-frame mountings, two outer and one centre: 81 Nm.Sigma reference: 19 mm socket and spanner; the guide lists two 19 mm sockets. Sigma pp. 108–109.
  7. Differential cage to differential: 81 Nm cap-head bolts and 47 Nm M10 bolt.Tool sizes unconfirmed: the Sigma differential section does not clearly document the same cage-to-differential arrangement. Sigma pp. 96–97.
  8. Differential cage to chassis: 81 Nm lower bolts and 54 Nm upper bolt.Sigma reference: 10 mm hex bit for the lower M12 cap-head bolts; 19 mm socket and spanner for the upper through-bolt. Verify the cage and fasteners match. Sigma pp. 96–97.
  9. Check wheel bearings rotate smoothly.

Steering
  1. Track rod ends to steering arms: 41 Nm.Tool size unconfirmed: the Sigma assembly tool list does not clearly identify this individual nut. Do not confuse it with the track-rod adjustment locknut. Sigma pp. 10–15.
  2. Steering rack clamp bolts: 11 Nm.Sigma reference: 5 mm Allen key / hex bit and 10 mm spanner for the M6 nuts. Sigma pp. 10–11.
  3. Check lower-to-upper steering column clamp bolts and grub screw, with no play.Partial Sigma reference: the assembly lists a 7/16 inch spanner, 7/32 inch hex bit, 13 mm tools and an 11/16 inch socket. Individual tool assignments are not all clear; verify each fixing. Sigma pp. 88–89.
  4. Check for no movement in the column bushes by moving the wheel up and down.

Engine
  1. Engine mounting rubbers to chassis: 20 Nm.Sigma engine reference only: 13 mm tools for M8 fixings. Confirm against the 2026 engine installation. Sigma pp. 40–41.
  2. Engine mounting rubbers to engine mounting brackets: 81 Nm.Sigma engine reference only: the assembly lists a ⅜ inch hex bit for the imperial cap-head fixing. Confirm against the 2026 engine installation. Sigma pp. 40–41.
  3. Engine mounting brackets to engine block: 25 Nm M8 bolts and 47 Nm M10 bolts.Sigma engine reference only: 6 mm hex bit for M8 cap-head bolts; 8 mm hex bit for M10 cap-head bolts. Confirm against the 2026 engine installation. Sigma pp. 40–41.
  4. Starter motor to engine: 47 Nm.Sigma engine reference only: 8 mm hex bit. Confirm the starter fixings on the 2026 engine. Sigma pp. 38–39.
  5. Air filter: check it is not dusty or dirty.
  6. Bellhousing to engine: 47 Nm.Sigma engine reference only: 8 mm hex bit. Confirm the bellhousing fixings on the 2026 engine. Sigma pp. 38–39.
  7. Check oil level with the engine warm, then switched off; wipe and refit the dipstick, wait 20 seconds, then read it.
  8. Ensure the dipstick is fully pushed home; the Driver Guide recommends securing it so it cannot come out on track.
  9. Check oil pressure behaviour: red at hot idle can be normal, but above 2000 rpm it should not stay in the red.

Gearbox
  1. Gearbox mount to gearbox: 54 Nm.Sigma gearbox reference only: 22 mm socket for its five-speed installation, or 19 mm for its six-speed installation. Confirm the 2026 gearbox and mount match. Sigma pp. 42–43.
  2. Gearbox mount to chassis: 20 Nm.Sigma gearbox reference only: 6 mm hex bit and 13 mm tools. Confirm the 2026 mount fixings. Sigma pp. 42–43.

Before Every Session

  • Torque the wheel nuts before every session: 85 Nm or 65 lb ft.Tool: 19 mm deep socket and torque wrench, as listed in the 2026 Driver Guide (p. 11).
  • Check oil level and wheel nuts again before going back on track.
  • Start each weekend with enough brake pad material to last. If in doubt, change pads before the final practice session.
  • Check the inner rear brake pads carefully; the Driver Guide notes they are likely to wear first.

Workshop position

Jacking And Axle Stands

Get the car supported correctly before working through the spanner checks. These notes are about where the car is lifted and supported, not the torque values themselves.

Support Points

  • Use the front towing eye as the front jacking point.
  • Use a trolley jack at the rear in the centre under the A-frame.
  • Use the correct front axle stand positions when spanner checking suspension.
  • Use the correct rear axle stand positions when spanner checking the rear of the car.
  • Do not use stand positions marked as not suitable for suspension tightening.
  • For suspension tightening checks, keep in mind that some checks are easier or more appropriate with the car on the floor.

Choosing tools

Tool Sizes And Sources

Tool sizes appear alongside the checks below. Torque settings remain from the 2026 Academy checklist; the Sigma assembly guide is used only to cross-reference tools and fasteners.

Sigma reference: match the illustrated fastener to your car before using the listed tool. Radius-arm compatibility has been confirmed by the owner. Other Sigma references remain to be checked on the 2026 car, especially engine, bellhousing, starter and gearbox fittings.

Partial reference: the guide lists tools for an assembly without identifying every individual fixing. Where the mapping is unclear, the check says so rather than assigning a size.

Open the Sigma assembly guide, Edition 2.2 (PDF). References below use printed page numbers; PDF viewer page numbers are nine higher. External PDFs require an internet connection.

Keep the Academy torque values. For example, the Sigma radius-arm chassis drawing gives 34 Nm, while the Academy checklist gives 47 Nm. Do not substitute the Sigma torque when using its tool reference. M8 and M10 describe thread sizes, not socket or spanner sizes.

Visual checks

Walkaround Checks

These are quick physical and visual checks to run alongside the spanner work.

Lighting
  • Check all lights are working.
  • Check all lights are fixed securely.
  • Check lights are taped where required.
  • Check rear bulbs are not touching the lenses.

Cooling System
  1. Visually check hose clips and connections for leaks.
  2. Check coolant level visually.
  3. Check radiator mountings and condition visually.

Wheels And Tyres
  1. Check tyre pressures.
  2. Check wheel weights are secure and taped.

Fibreglass
  1. Check front wing fixings.
  2. Check rear wing fixings.
  3. Check nose cone fasteners.
  4. Check the grille is secure in the nose cone.

Other Checks
  1. Check race numbers and sponsor logos.
  2. Clean the car.
  3. Check brake pad material.
  4. Check that you have good pedal feel.

Workshop notes

Top Tips

Practical notes from the supplied checklist. Treat these as prompts to check against your own car and current championship guidance.

Tools And Access

  • Cut down an 8 mm Allen key for easier access on the radius arms.
  • Do not use impact guns or impact wrenches.
  • Use a trolley jack under the front towing eye and at the rear in the centre under the A-frame.

Basic Workshop Kit

  • Low-profile trolley jack rated for at least 1.5 tonnes.
  • Three axle stands so the car can be supported level for spanner checks.
  • Torque wrench capable of more than 85 Nm, with a 19 mm deep socket for wheel nuts.
  • Digital tyre pressure gauge, ideally with a flexible hose.
  • Tyre pump or inflator.
  • Jerry can or cans with a funnel or spout.
  • Bucket, sponge, car shampoo, leather, and polish.
  • Small socket set and screwdriver set for trackside fixings.

Consumables To Carry

  • 50 mm gaffer tape for the windscreen-to-roll-cage gap.
  • Headlight tape, avoiding red and blue.
  • 50 mm anti-slip tape for both impact bars.
  • Cable ties for hoses, cycle wings, and temporary trackside fixes.
  • Brake cleaner and water for cleaning up brake fluid.
  • Glass cleaner and Rain-X for the windscreen and visor, inside and outside.
  • Workshop cloths, nitrile gloves, and disposable wipes.
  • Engine oil, coolant, brake fluid, and a funnel or spout.
  • Sweatband for the race master cylinder cap.

Trackside Fixes

  • Carry cable ties for the cycle wings in case one is lost or needs temporary securing.
  • Side repeaters are not required on race cars.
  • If the windscreen is damaged on the driver's side, it can be rotated as a workaround before replacement.

Driver And Session Reminders

  • Keep a short list on the dash for session reminders.
  • Use Rain-X on the windscreen and visor, inside and outside.

Brake And Fluid Notes

  • Fit the race master cylinder cap, 77175, and use a sweatband to catch seepage.
  • Buy a brake bleed kit or similar.
  • Clean up brake fluid with water.

Workshop Notes

  • Do not over-torque wheel nuts; over-torque can stretch studs and make future loosening more likely.
  • The Driver Guide notes that copper grease on wheel studs can help with frequent wheel removal; keep any grease away from braking surfaces.
  • Release the torque wrench back to its storage setting at the end of the day.
